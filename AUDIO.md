# Audio implementation and verification

This document describes `continuum-20.0.0`. Actual measured runs, including previous implementations, are recorded separately in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md). The current architecture does not retain separate manual, unphrased, exploratory and lyrical generators behind its controls.

Version 20 consolidates the dormant generators and routes thematic, harmonic and contrapuntal choices through the same occurrence-based vocabulary plan. `npm run audit:tools` checks heard uses and deferred opportunities; merely registering a tool or proposing a candidate does not count as hearing it. Fixed-span duration scaling that would cancel during timing normalization is excluded from occurrence coverage. Source augmentation/diminution remains audible through allocated clause durations.

The score uses scoped idea DAGs, continuous multiscale formal spectra, exponential subdivision preferences, complete evaluated counterpoint and declarative player policies. Version 19 adds a shared gesture algebra for source invention and development, short active arguments, and directed refinement of written anchors. Ornament steps can refine to an integral descendant of the texture subdivision when a short connected link needs room. Backing follows the actual thought origin and endpoint, including thoughts that begin inside a section bar.

`SoundingScore` retains physical line history for both harmony and counterpoint, clips replaced sources without resurrecting their tails, and preserves explicit or implicit glide timing through rests. Counterpoint integrates sounding pitch over its overlap rather than scoring only the pitch at the original attack. Rhythmic orchestration reads actual shared accents, including tuplets, and does not select future or superseded harmonic sources. Expression audition regions are selected jointly so one example cannot consume the only real rise or retreat available for another.

Version 17 connects source grouping, goal-directed melody, rhythm subdivision, chordal bass figures and prepared fills through a shared idea kernel. Metrical accents remain stronger than their surrounding detail. The production melody uses one source-graph compiler across density levels; its identifiable head and held destinations receive readable pulse positions. Meter residences span several complete arguments instead of changing to exhaust a catalogue. With **Tuning journeys & glides** enabled, source-addressed connection windows also articulate interior upper-voice and foreground ornament transitions. Structural melody anchors stay exact; ordinary legato still does not slide implicitly. Additive playback retains its matching static spectrum, and standard MIDI still explicitly rejects gliding/microtonal performances.

Version 16 distinguishes ordinary legato from a composed pitch slide. Connected notes reach their exact native pitch at the written onset while retaining oscillator phase and a held amplitude body. Phrase cues and repeated structural notes can reaccent that body; ordinary core continuations keep their previous peak. Foreground gain curves now distinguish arrivals, continuations and closing releases. These changes make the written phrasing more explicit without adding random timing variation or changing the matched additive reference.

Version 15 composes several melodic contour families and clause operations, with open handoffs or settled endings. Multi-section journeys prepare larger changes; expression curves retain rate bounds and continuity except at declared cuts. A fixed-phase reference pulse coexists with separately introduced riffs and responses. Native 19-, 24- and 31-EDO use one physical pitch boundary and bounded native candidate search, with explicit musical preferences per tuning. The ordinary ensemble and matched additive rack both retain those fractional pitches.

The **Render composition studies** laboratory action renders six common-engine recipes at the same seed and tempo. It uses complete first thematic arguments, not selected favorable peaks, and isolates the first four leads. A separate first-four-argument cohort measures later ornament and clause development. Reports include exact event replay, actual onset gaps and directional steps, reference-pulse phase, instrument colors, fractional pitch use, source gates and full PCM scans. A zero ornament count in a spacious window is shown honestly. Ornament unit tests alone are insufficient: production tests also require emitted ornament events with protected core identities.

Version 14 separates actual idea density and ensemble size from activity and intensity. Whole authored arguments can become sparse or contain diminished subjects, sequences, scale connections and register tours. `RhythmicScore` supplies the common onset/rest cell for drums, bass and comping. `arrangement.ts` realizes one to twelve pitched players from that material, with scoped source releases and matched MIDI tracks. The additive reference bed remains exempt. Flute, brass and synth lead join the existing six oscillator colors; connected wind/lead lines use bounded native-frequency legato, and each physical line replaces its prior overlapping hold. Nine explicit color spectra remain lightweight, with at most four oscillators each.

The range audit on `/audio-audit.html` renders four equal-length readings of the same seed and tempo: sparse/dense solo and sparse/dense ensemble. It measures actual PCM, written lead density, register range, shared attacks, player participation and exact foreground identity across orchestration changes. Expression auditions select low/high/rising/falling energy regions independently and show their actual chronological order. Low energy does not imply sparse melody or a large held string bed. The separate gain-envelope reference identifies which selected excerpt supplies its sustained material.

## From composition to sound

Every engine resolves one composition profile. `ScoreTimeline` combines the conductor with `compositionExpressionAt`, then applies explicit automation last. A protected `ThemeCore` supplies the melodic argument. `HarmonicDirector` chooses destinations against its held and structurally important notes, and the native planner places those identities in the upper voices and bass. Thematic delivery, accompaniment, groove, related counterline and prepared transitions use the same expressive reading.

Dynamic breadth is independent of autonomous macro freedom. The shared reading combines a long intensity arc with shorter activity breaths, register motion, sustain and accent. It changes actual attack spacing and articulation as well as amplitude. Calm upper voices hold the current destination; flowing passages distribute connected tones; crests develop a shared pulse across the backing. Structural bass roots are guaranteed at harmonic boundaries, and moving bass contours use the current native sonority. Old chord holds end at the next destination.

Core pitch and rhythm identities are separate from delivery. Native-scale links may fill suitable continuations, while the head, prepared apex and cadence remain protected. Event `expression` metadata names source identities, anchors, ornaments and supports. Synthesis uses the actual pitches, times, gates and velocities; metadata alone is not evidence that a planned event sounded. The independent answer quotes the theme on its own clock and leaves quiet passages open.

`ensemble.ts` coordinates selected entries, accents and arrivals using the current upper and bass templates. Calm coordination leaves most sustained upper voices intact. Shared breaks constrain every relevant part; pitched punctuation is skipped during tuning glides. Fills and counterlines enter before the final orchestral gain and onset ceiling, so they cannot bypass a retreat's dynamic policy. Full dynamic breadth maps the shared intensity to a gain from 0.48 to 1.75, centered at 1; zero breadth applies gain 1. Instrument levels, note velocities, envelopes, density and compression also affect audible loudness, so these values are not PCM-level targets.

## Scheduling, pauses and compatibility

`src/audio.ts` separates synthesis from generation. `AudioPlayer` runs a 25 ms scheduler and admits chronological onset, rest and boundary tasks into a 400 ms audio-clock window. Source graphs are created incrementally instead of constructing a dense frame at once. A frame is announced when audible; the UI defers DOM work to animation frames and skips offscreen rendering. Bounded form, parameter and texture caches do not change musical decision order.

Pause suspends the audio clock, including queued notes and effect tails. Resume continues that position. Stop clears the queue and invalidates pending resume/timer callbacks. `getOutputTimestamp()` aligns observation with speaker output when available, with a latency fallback; scheduling uses the render clock. Late planning shifts a new boundary forward instead of scheduling in the past. A scheduler exception stops once and calls `onError`. `navigation.ts` reconstructs history and buffers the destination frame, keeping seeks from skipping a phrase. Live playback and rendered previews are mutually exclusive.

Envelope cancellation is capability-tested. If `AudioParam.cancelAndHoldAtTime` is unavailable, a bounded record of scheduled set, ramp and target events computes the value at the requested boundary. Cancellation and a replacement ramp endpoint preserve the incoming envelope. This covers sustained sources, rests, glides and output fades. Cleanup errors cannot hide the original scheduling failure. An externally suspended context becomes a visible paused state; closing the device reports a reconnectable failure.

Musical note ends are retained as ticks. Each committed tempo boundary retimes crossing oscillator stops, gain envelopes and pitch trajectories without generating unknown future frames. Live and offline rendering use the same boundary sequence. Drum hits retain their fixed timbral decays; natural pluck decay limits are also distinct from symbolic note-off time.

## Ensemble instruments and expressive events

The sample-free ensemble provides keys, glass, reed, pluck, round bass, strings, flute, brass and synth lead. Sustained, connected and detached articulation is independent of instrument color. Sustained notes use a soft attack and held body, including when the chosen color ordinarily decays quickly. Strings provide a stable long-note foreground. Eligible overlapping same-color foreground notes reuse one oscillator bank with phase continuity, but switch to the exact written frequency at onset. Legato does not insert an undeclared pitch slide. Written gaps release and reattack; color changes select a new voice.

A cue-bearing anchor or a repeated-pitch anchor receives a bounded reaccent, followed by a smooth early settling into the held body. Its peak never exceeds the event's velocity multiplied by the existing instrument level. Other connected notes retain their prior peak. This also removes the former late amplitude step near note-off. Explicit pitch trajectories, musical rests, source cleanup and the missing-`cancelAndHoldAtTime` fallback retain their own timing policies.

Identical overlapping upper notes can tie. A new ensemble bass attack replaces the previous bass with a 35 ms crossfade, retaining repeated-note rearticulation. New upper attacks use bounded replacement fades rather than accumulating indefinite held sources. Ordinary upper attacks may receive deterministic millisecond offsets; explicit supports, written gain trajectories and glides bypass this offset to preserve coordinated timing. Synthesis randomness has separate addresses from composition.

The ensemble rack includes warmth filtering, stereo placement, a fixed seeded impulse, filtered delay and gentle compression. Noise percussion reaches the compressor after the warmth filter: hats retain a 4.6–12 kHz band, while snares combine band-passed noise with a short drum-head tone. These timbral choices are not the additive roughness reference.

Optional `gainEnvelope` points use integer offsets from onset and gains in `[0,1]`. An independent gain stage follows the piecewise-linear curve without replacing the instrument's own envelope. At a tempo boundary, the current value is held and future points are retimed. `note-expression.ts` clips a shortened note at the interpolated endpoint rather than squeezing the entire curve into a shorter span. This allows a held tone to grow or recede without repeated attacks.

Foreground notes long enough for a gain contour use their compositional role: structural leaps of at least 400 cents and declared entries/arrivals bloom earlier and settle; closed cadences taper; interior continuations carry the larger shared intensity curve with less per-note shaping. Four musical-time knots occur at 0%, 22%, 68% and 100% of the written duration. Each role curve multiplies the shared intensity reading, stays at or below one, and preserves the written velocity ceiling. The same late crescendo is no longer imposed on every note.

Notes can also declare `endPitch` and `glideTicks`. Oscillator frequency ramps exponentially, producing linear motion in cents. Intermediate pitches remain physical frequencies, and native 19-, 24- and 31-EDO destinations are never rounded to MIDI semitones. A glide cannot disappear into common-tone tying. The expressive glide stage connects selected nearby upper destinations and eligible non-anchor links; protected theme anchors stay literal. Tuning journeys use the same physical pitch contract. Their diagnostics describe the destination sonority, not a time-integrated glide spectrum.

Explicit rests interrupt dry sources at their musical boundary. Foreground breaths name their affected voices, allowing an independent answer to continue where intended. Accompaniment rests cover harmony, bass and percussion; ensemble rests cover all parts. `restAppliesToNote` is shared by event clipping, synthesis and audit stems. Short source fades prevent clicks, while effects decay naturally. A written breath therefore does not require exact digital zero and never pauses the transport.

## Matched additive instrument

The additive path is one instrument adapter within the same composition. It retains a continuous four-upper-plus-bass reference through each structural harmonic window, ending the old bed when the destination changes. It bypasses faster pitched accompaniment figures, additional pitched coordination/counterline attacks, velocity scaling, held gain swells and ensemble spectral effects. Thematic melody, formal development, explicit rests and native tuning journeys remain part of the score.

Every pitched additive tone uses the exact configured five sine partial ratios and amplitudes, a uniform note level and a common reference envelope. The bank bypasses ensemble filters, stereo placement, reverb, delay and compression. Nonzero velocity does not change its equal-note reference amplitude; zero-velocity notes remain silent. The overall volume still applies. Percussion and foreground melody are outside the static harmonic-bed model, so a roughness score is not a measurement of the entire mix.

The model evaluates actual-Hz interactions between all unordered partial pairs, including within-tone intrinsic roughness. It uses the critical-band scaling and minimum-amplitude weighting from [Sethares' published implementation](https://sethares.engr.wisc.edu/comprog.html), with background in [Relating Tuning and Timbre](https://sethares.engr.wisc.edu/consemi.html). `raw / (raw + 5)` is a fixed engineering scale. It is separate from harmonic function, expectation, multidimensional tension and musical quality. Register, spectrum and tuning can change the model's preference; minimizing roughness everywhere is not the compositional objective.

Native 19-, 24- and 31-EDO composition uses explicit integer-degree fields and transformations. Physical ranges and separation constraints are shared; declared collections, triads and region cycles are specific choices. The 24-EDO polarity uses complementary 450/250-cent thirds. P/R/L relations preserve common tones, but their native labels do not assert classical tonal function. These are native continuations, not retuned 12-TET output or a universal tuning theory. The [theme study](public/theme-study.html#living-lines) preserves the related research and its limitations.

## Rendering and audits

`renderOffline(frames, volume)` renders committed history through the same rack, integrates its tempo map, and appends effect-tail time. `encodeWav` writes stereo 16-bit PCM. Browser implementations can differ in waveform samples even when committed musical events agree. Event replay, signal correctness and subjective listening are separate questions.

The main **Render theme comparison** provides the complete core alone and with functional, common-tone and third-cycle readings. It checks source identity, physical melody pitches and timing, and reports actual distinct accompaniments. **Render this section** exposes the current arrangement as a separate preview. **Render A / B audition** compares a fixed recipe with different sensory influence. These tools use the chosen tuning and production synthesizer; a lower target error is not a listening verdict.

The development `/audio-audit.html` includes:

- General signal renders with finite/nonfinite counts, peak, RMS, clipping, DC and tail measurements.
- Full-theme mix, foreground and backing players, keeping the complete argument and nearby context.
- **Hear melodic gestures and verify legato**: an actual opening argument as a full mix and isolated foreground, plus controlled native legato and explicit-glide players. The report separates foreground voice 5 from top harmony voice 3 and measures the first 128 committed frames: physical intervals, gate and onset-gap distributions, velocities, articulation, written breaths and leap-target relationships. Short-window spectral measurements check immediate arrival at the ordinary legato target and continuing motion during a declared glide. Passing signal or frequency checks does not establish musical quality.
- Calm, rise, crest and retreat excerpts, with contextual held notes and a flattened-gain reference for comparison.
- Composition and layer arrangements that inspect protected source identities, added ornaments, coordinated attacks, cycles and planned transitions.
- Articulation and percussion probes at controlled pitch/velocity.
- Native 19-, 24- and 31-EDO FFT checks against requested frequencies and partial amplitude vectors, plus pitch journeys and tempo-boundary sustain checks.
- A live scheduling probe reporting audio-clock progress, context state, frame counts, planning/scheduling time, pump gaps, late tasks, source counts and analyser levels. Its compatibility option exercises the missing-native-hold fallback through real Web Audio and restores the method afterward.

Audit helpers expose rendered buffers through callbacks for actual players/downloads. They do not modify the instrument's live recipe or storage. Stem renders aid comparison but do not exactly sum to a nonlinear compressed full mix. Planned transition gestures may be shortened by higher-priority shared breaks; the panel labels them **Planned** rather than claiming every proposed attack survives.

`npm run audit:integrated` examines the shared production pipeline without Web Audio. `npm run audit:harmony` measures native destination realization, melody identity, actual voice movement and replay. Other audit scripts retain the history of earlier experiments and should be interpreted in their version context. Browser signal measurements and listening are needed in addition to event audits. Held-gain proxies omit oscillator spectra, instrument envelopes, compression, effects and physical replacement fades; they are not loudness meters.

`npx tsx scripts/audit-melodic-intent.ts <output.json>` runs the same 128-frame score measurements for glass-garden, velvet-orbit and amber-current across Developing solo, Interlocking drive and Floating lines. It records source-file hashes before and after generation so a concurrent edit cannot silently masquerade as a stable baseline. Its connection counts are eligible score relationships, not measured oscillator allocations. The browser laboratory supplies the separate PCM evidence and listening players; `VALIDATION.md` records which runs were actually performed.

Conformance uses reviewed, fixed event hashes shared by Node and browser runs. Expected values are not recomputed during an audit. Version 20 changes evaluated development, harmonic routes, counterpoint and ornament choices; earlier recipes are preserved before UI reinterpretation and are not claimed to replay their older events. [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md) records final runs and retains older evidence under its original version headings.

## Export boundaries

MIDI export writes type 1 files at 480 PPQ with separate parts, velocities, tempo and meter changes, section markers and instrument programs. Interval exports clip and rebase notes while inheriting the tempo at their start. Repeated overlapping equal pitches on a track are reconciled so a late note-off cannot cut a newer event; bass rearticulation remains explicit.

Standard MIDI rejects native tunings, fractional pitches, tuning journeys and declared glides instead of rounding them. Baseline MIDI does not encode held gain curves as controller data. Performance JSON preserves the full deterministic recipe, and rendered WAV preserves the synthesized result. A successful encoder test does not prove a browser download completed; export observations are listed separately in the validation record.
